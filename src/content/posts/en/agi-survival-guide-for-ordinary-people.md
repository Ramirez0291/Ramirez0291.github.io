And I think if we do this right, we can create an age of miracles where humanity is able to solve problems that have eluded us for millennia.  ----Dario Amodei

From 2024 to 2025, AI finished off programming. Then it finished off end-to-end knowledge work. Now? Fundamental breakthroughs in biology, mathematics, and physics are already happening.

Within a decade, AI could compress fifty to one hundred years of medical progress into thin air, turning cancer and Alzheimer's into history——or so he claims.

I know there's definitely some pre-IPO fundraising fantasy baked into this: even if raw compute could overnight screen millions of antibody molecules and dream up brilliant experimental strategies and breakthrough paths, human drug metabolism, seven to eight year clinical trials, and regulatory approval still can't be GPU-accelerated. Even Turing laureate Yann LeCun and plenty of other scholars keep reminding everyone not to mistake a really useful tool for a newly awakened species.

But watching Cody Tranbarger coordinate 11 external labs on Claude alone and get a rare muscle disease drug to successful mouse trials in under 18 months? Plus what Fable 5.2 just pulled off these past few days——absolutely wild stuff. I still want to believe the fantasy.

When you actually use it you can feel it's straitjacketed by safety guardrails so tight it can barely walk, that same nervous whisper in its voice as GPT6-Astra. Yet even through all that bulletproof glass, I can feel an absurdly powerful creature locked behind that door. It's the second time in my life I've genuinely sensed something you could call 'creativity' starting to grow on silicon.

Watching AI storm city after city in mathematical proofs, cutting-edge research, biotech, even embodied robots——whether real AGI lands three years from now or doesn't show up for a decade, how should ordinary people like me—the kind you'd grab by the dozen off the street——actually live through this era?

## 1. First: stay alive and hang on until the day the "Cyber Hippocrates" starts taking your health insurance

Look, right now it doesn't matter if you're reading roadshow decks or listening to executives talk—everyone goes on about compute, valuations, intelligence emergence. But here's the blunt truth: none of it means anything to us ordinary people unless you're actually here to witness it.

Dario Amodei's father died from hepatitis C years back. Then just a few years later, a new antiviral drug hit the market with a 95% cure rate.

Think about it: if AI really does crack cancer, Alzheimer's, and all those freakish incurable diseases in ten years, turning them into the medical equivalent of treating a cold, and then you check out before the day new drugs finish their marathon clinical trials, hit affordable prices, and ordinary people get to pop a shot through their health plan——wouldn't that be the absolute worst L?

Here's what I think: the first thing ordinary people should learn preparing for AGI isn't studying documentation. It's getting decent sleep. Skip the sugary boba. Go to the gym after work. Stop ignoring those arrows on your checkup reports. Since we can't fast-forward the actual time clinical trials take, we shouldn't try to outpace algorithms on iteration speed. We need to compete on meat-body depreciation rate. As long as your body's still running, hang on until the day AI solves every terminal illness and makes healthcare cheap enough for ordinary people——that's winning the first half.

## 2. Stop being a human connector cable. Go do what you're actually supposed to do.

Here's what stings: one person plus a bunch of AIs can coordinate 11 biotech labs doing drug research, so why do office towers still have all these people every day doing pure manual Excel slavery? Why do we still have to be a "human connector" with Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V between two crumbling ancient systems that should be in a museum? Even more absurd: everyone wastes hours in meetings doing office politics, arguing about whether headings are off by a few pixels or fighting over indentation, getting swindled by info-gap middlemen who've built empires on language barriers.

Don't waste your life on this stuff.

The tools are sitting right there. You can throw all the garbage work at AI. Two legacy systems don't talk to each other? Get AI to whip up a couple dozen lines of automation to wire them together.

Can't read cutting-edge papers from across the ocean? Toss them straight to the model for real-time translation, cross-check, and go look at the original truth yourself.

As for data cleaning, format alignment——all that mechanical labor? Silicon does it orders of magnitude better than carbon.

What we need to do is use AI as an amplifier for our own talents and spend the time we save making real decisions that actually need human judgment.

## 3. Go accumulate those "quirks" algorithms can't compute. Do what you're supposed to do as a human.

Since we can't keep up with problem sets, memorization, writing clean code, or even reviewing foundational research——silicon's beating carbon on every single track——we should just stop racing those races.

Train your taste. Train your aesthetic sense. Train your imagination and creativity. In other words, train the parts that make humans human.

Even a powerhouse like Fable 5.2 is still a prim AI young lady locked behind safety guardrails and probability distributions. It can zip through every movie script in human history, but it doesn't know why the rusty iron smell in the air at 5:30pm during rainy season suddenly makes you want to hear some old song from twenty years ago; it can paint a cat with every hair obeying the laws of optics, but it can't know what it feels like to bury your face in that sun-dried old quilt from your grandmother's house.

Whether something looks good, whether a song is right for you, whether an idea will blow up——it all comes down to the intuition worn sharp from the stories you've lived, books you read for no reason, rains you got caught in. Go waste time daydreaming in museums. Read pointless books. Develop your own quirks. The more you sharpen those human edges——your rough spots, your idiosyncrasies, your capacity to hurt——in an age full of safety guardrails and standard answers, the more irreplaceable you become.

## 4. Don't put all your chips on a single plank.

Every time a tech wave crashes, especially right now when Anthropic's sprinting toward a trillion-dollar IPO and every large model and embodied robot is flooding feeds, remember: don't go all-in chasing trends. A couple years back people were pushing everyone to day-trade crypto full-time. These days it's telling people to blow their savings on compute stocks, or forcing kids barely out of elementary school to spend eight hours a day coding, like if you don't ALL-IN on AI and chips tomorrow you'll be picking cardboard boxes off the street by next week.

Don't believe it. Not even if the sector is AI. Not even if it's chips.

Anthropic's IPO valuation dropped from their initial 2 trillion dollar float just a few months back down to 1 to 1.4 trillion. When everyone's jumping into the same pit, even if there's really gold in there, you'll get trampled. Besides, tech trends change faster than flipping a page——two years ago companies were hiring "prompt engineers" at premium wages, then the model just figured out reasoning on its own.

The most stable way for ordinary people to live has always been multiple escape routes. Keep your day job solid. Learn some skill totally unrelated to your main work in your spare time. Keep cash on hand you can pull out anytime. Have friends you can grab for good food even if the internet dies. Don't push all your chips onto the same table——even if the table gets flipped tomorrow, you can brush yourself off and go have tea at the next café.

If you really don't know what to invest in, just buy Coca-Cola. Even if AI overlords take over the planet tomorrow, even if Nvidia goes bankrupt, humans will always need that little sweet drink dopamine hit.

## 5. Since the spade's already in your hand, dig whenever you feel like it.

We used to have so many excuses to talk ourselves out of our dumb ideas.

"I have this brilliant idea but I don't know how to code." "I wanted to start a cross-border business but my English isn't good enough." "I want to compile my parents' memories into a book, but transcription takes forever."

But now it's different. Before, from "sudden inspiration" to "having something that actually works"——between them sat professional barriers, language walls, and long trial-and-error costs. Now there's just a dialog box between you and that.

Company's two clunky systems are a pain? Grab AI and throw together a little plugin to hack it private. Want to read the latest papers or industry gossip from across the ocean? Let AI break the language wall for you. Got a weird story bouncing around in your head? An indie game concept? Some hardware gadget? Don't wait until next year——fire up your computer right now and build it with AI.

In the AGI era, the friction between ideas and reality has dropped almost to zero. The only thing that separates you is whether you've got the guts to pull yourself out of pointless document alignment meetings and office squabbles and roll up your sleeves the moment a dumb idea pops into your head.

Because when we really get to the point where embodied robots and AI overlords are everywhere, when ordinary people gather to drink and shoot the shit, we're not gonna swap war stories about "I was really good at copy-pasting between two systems" or "I could tie up a travel reimbursement form for a whole month," right?
